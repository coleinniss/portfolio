Monarch Money Mobile: Expert Review

Monarch Money Heuristic Evaluation, Part 2 Expert Review
Project context

My goal of running this heuristics to to further understand how to use Nielsen’s 10 usability heuristics with specificity. I ran an evaluation with the perspective of analyzing for autistic individuals. I found research documents indicating best practices, listed below. A lot of the points in the checklist is well geared for this sort of task.

Heuristic tasks

Task A

Set up a budget and goals as a first-time budgeter with no existing goals.

Task B

Create a savings goal and decide how much to put toward it each month.

What research says about data visualization for autistic people

01

Wu, Petersen, Ahmad, Burlinson, Tanis & Albers Szafir (2021). Understanding Data Accessibility for People with Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities. CHI ’21. doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445743

  • Participants with IDD were significantly more accurate with stacked bars and tree maps as with pie charts.
  • Bar charts worked better than line graphs for finding highs and lows.
How it applies to Monarch

Supports bar charts for the flexible-number breakdown, and keeping charts plain instead of adding icons or decoration.

02

Wu, Tran, Petersen, Koushik & Albers Szafir (2023). Data, Data, Everywhere: Uncovering Everyday Data Experiences for People with Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities. CHI ’23.

  • Participants had trouble recalling and combining information across screens and apps, and wanted step-by-step formats tied to real goals.
  • Autistic participants described overload and overstimulation from dense data displays.
How it applies to Monarch

Supports Findings 1 and 2 (remembering terms and numbers across screens) and the case for calmer screens without decorative photos.

How I applied the heuristics for autistic users

  1. #1

    Visibility of System Status

    No surprises. The app shows what changed and why.

  2. #2

    Match Between System and Real World

    Literal, concrete words. No idioms or metaphors.

  3. #4

    Consistency and Standards

    The same word or color means the same thing everywhere.

  4. #6

    Recognition Rather than Recall

    Information stays on screen instead of being remembered.

  5. #8

    Aesthetic and Minimalist Design

    Calm screens that show only what the current step needs.

Final artifacts
Key design moments

Findings

Finding 1 · Task A

The budget groups aren't explained where you need them

Heuristics #2, #6
Problem

For screens 5-6 there is three options of type of budgeting plan: Fixed, Non-Monthly and Flexible. Which is great but the definitions is only accessible if they press back or “Here.”

Why it matters for autistic users

Having to remember definitions from an earlier screen adds memory load, and a link that only says "Here" doesn't tell you where it goes.

Finding 2 · Task A

The flexible number doesn't add up

Heuristics #1, #6
Problem

I selected flexible payment and it shows $0. Now when you select categories, naturally you’d think the more you select it’ll add up but it doesn’t change (check screen 10-11).

Why it matters for autistic users

The label says one thing and the numbers say another. For someone who reads labels literally, that can make the whole budget feel untrustworthy.

Finding 3 · Task B

Goals change the budget without telling you

Heuristics #1, #3
Problem

For screen 19-20, I was setting up a goal, it asked for $834 a month. However there isn’t an indicator, how does that work and look?

Why it matters for autistic users

Hidden effects make the app less predictable. Seeing the result before saving removes the guesswork.

Finding 4 · Task A

Red bars for normal spending

Heuristics #2, #4
Problem

For screen 8, I was editing my Internet & Cable budget and every past month showed up as a bright red bar, even September at $61, which is under my $75 plan.

Why it matters for autistic users

Bright red is a strong warning signal and can feel alarming or overstimulating. When it shows up for normal spending, it adds stress and teaches you to ignore the color.

Finding 5 · Task B

An "optional" field says it's required

Heuristics #4, #5
Problem

There was a weird interaction, screen 16-17, the target date was optional but it was actually required? Not sure what happened.

Why it matters for autistic users

Two labels that contradict each other force you to guess which one is true.

Impact

Takeaways for accessible finance tools

Overall, Monarch Money is passes a lot of case heuristics. I am curious about other parts of the application, they have very accessible graphs, this is great for people interested in seeing how their money moves visually. The text throughout the onboarding, budgeting and goal setting is straightforward. It follows a lot of rules for accessible language, does miss parts of finer details but I think someone reporting the application it’ll get get cleaned up. I recommend it, I might start using it myself actually.

Reflections

Limitations: Not a lot of graphs in the evaluations (much less than I thought!), and I am not autistic but interested in the DID related data visualizations.
